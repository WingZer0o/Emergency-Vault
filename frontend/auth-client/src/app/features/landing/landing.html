How it works
01

Set up the vault

A master key is derived from your password in the browser and never transmitted. It wraps a keypair generated on your device. You are shown a 32-byte recovery key once — the only other way in.

02

Record what matters

Institution logins, account numbers, POA instructions, and PDF or DOCX attachments — each encrypted client-side under your vault key before upload.

03

Name who gets in

Grant access to an executor, attorney, or spouse by email. Your vault key is re-wrapped to their public key, and released to them only after they verify a one-time code.

What zero-knowledge means here

A subpoena to us produces base64 and nothing else.

Every secret is encrypted before it is sent. The server stores opaque blobs, a public key, and a KDF salt. It has no derivation path to your plaintext.

Expand any line for the exact construction.

Your password derives a key, not a credential

PBKDF2-SHA256 over 600,000 iterations with a per-vault 16-byte salt produces a non-extractable AES-256 master key. It exists only in memory, only long enough to unwrap your private key, and is never sent anywhere.

Items are sealed with authenticated encryption

Each item and each attachment is encrypted with AES-256-GCM under your vault key, with a fresh 96-bit IV. A tampered blob fails its authentication tag rather than decrypting to garbage.

Sharing is post-quantum hybrid, not a link

To grant access, your vault key is wrapped in an envelope that combines RSA-OAEP-3072 with ML-KEM-768 through an HKDF-SHA256 combiner. Both must break for the envelope to open — so traffic captured today is not readable by a future quantum attacker.

Recovery is a key you hold, not a reset we perform

Setup generates 32 random bytes shown once as grouped hex. It wraps a second copy of your private key. Support cannot substitute for it; print it and store it with the will.

What we do see

Attachment file names, MIME types and sizes are stored in the clear so the format allowlist can be enforced server-side. Item timestamps and the identities of people you share with are also visible to us. We state this plainly rather than claim otherwise.
